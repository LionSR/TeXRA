\section{Introduction}%


Aim of the paper: 

Preparing entangled states or ground states of particular Hamiltonians is a highly relevant topic in quantum information processing and condensed matter and quantum many-body physics. We propose here two different approaches, which are less conventional than preparation via quantum circuits. To this end, we consider a system whose evolution might be governed by some particular Hamiltonian, $\hat{H}_S$, being coupled to a bath. We use this interaction to design an evolution whose stationary state is close to the ground state of the Hamiltonian, $\hat{H}_S$, or more generally to the desired state. We derive two algorithms to achieve this task. First, we consider dissipative state preparation, where the evolution of the system due to the Hamiltonian of interest is not required, i.e., $\hat{H}_S$ can be chosen to vanish, and second, a cooling algorithm, where the evolution is essential for the process to work. We derive both algorithms for the Ising model and show that both of them can be utilized to prepare the ground state. However, we also show that the cooling algorithm works better in case one considers various different parameters in $\hat{H}_S$. Next, we show how the algorithms can be improved by considering various different couplings and settings. Finally, we show that even in the presence of noise, the cooling and dissipative state preparation algorithms work.
